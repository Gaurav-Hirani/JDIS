# JDIS Prediction UI & Calibration Note

This document clarifies the distinction between raw model probabilities and calibrated probabilities in the JDIS prediction pipeline, and why the UI explicitly distinguishes them.

## Raw Probability
The **raw probability** is the direct output from the XGBoost classification model. This represents the model's uncalibrated confidence that a case will exceed 24 months of delay based purely on the decision trees. Raw probabilities are rarely true probabilities and often suffer from overconfidence or underconfidence.

## Calibrated Probability
The **calibrated probability** is the operational probability used by JDIS. It is generated by passing the raw XGBoost output through an Isotonic Regression calibrator that was strictly fitted on the historical 2015 validation cohort. This transforms the raw output into a statistically reliable probability that matches the true historical delay frequency.

## Isotonic Stepwise Behavior
Because Isotonic Regression is a non-parametric method that fits a monotonic non-decreasing piecewise constant function, the resulting mapping is **stepwise**. This means that a continuous range of different raw probabilities can be mapped to the exact same calibrated probability step.

**Example Scenario**:
- Case A: Raw Probability = 0.8494
- Case B: Raw Probability = 0.8723
Due to the stepwise nature of the isotonic curve for that interval, both map to a Calibrated Probability = 0.8129.

## Risk Score Formula
The final JDIS Risk Score is derived deterministically from the calibrated probability, per the frozen ML research specification:
`risk_score = floor(calibrated_probability * 100)`

Thus, a calibrated probability of 0.8129 yields a Risk Score of **81**.

## UI Transparency
The frontend prediction result interface explicitly displays both the **Raw Model Probability** and the **Calibrated Delay Probability**. This transparency ensures that users (such as faculty and analysts during demonstrations) understand that the underlying case features are actively generating unique raw predictions, even if those predictions fall onto the same calibration step and result in identical final Risk Scores.
