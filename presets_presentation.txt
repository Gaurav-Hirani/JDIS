HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT
================================================================================

Each preset below is a realistic case record entered into the JDIS UI.
JDIS predicts the probability that this case will exceed 24 months from
filing. The output is:

  - Calibrated Probability: 0.00 – 1.00   (continuous risk estimate)
  - Risk Score:               0 – 100     (floor(prob × 100))
  - Risk Band:                Low / Moderate / High / Very High
  - Top SHAP Drivers:         which features pushed the prediction up or down

Risk Band Thresholds:
  Low        = 0 – 20
  Moderate   = 21 – 50
  High       = 51 – 80
  Very High  = 81 – 100

ACTUAL Band Distribution (from live model run, 2026-10-19):
  Low        → Presets 1, 2                              (score 0)
  Moderate   → Preset 3                                  (score 41)
  High       → Presets 4, 6, 7, 8                        (scores 57, 57, 75, 75)
  Very High  → Presets 5, 9–20                           (scores 81–100)

Counts: 2 Low, 1 Moderate, 4 High, 13 Very High

Note on the distribution: JDIS is trained on 29 administrative features and
predicts whether a case exceeds 24 months. Most case types in the training
data genuinely do exceed 24 months, so the calibrated probabilities for
in-distribution inputs are concentrated in the High/Very High range. The Low
and Moderate presets (bail applications and juvenile cases) are the
differentiating showcases — they demonstrate the model outputs a meaningful
range across the full 0–100 scale, not just a flat high score.

PRESENTATION TALKING POINTS
================================================================================

1. FULL 0–100 RANGE DEMONSTRATED
   - Preset 1 (bail app, Bhusawal) = 0   → lowest possible risk
   - Preset 12 (declarative suits, Nandurbar, backlog 10,200) = 100
   → highest possible risk
   - Preset 3 (juvenile, Bhusawal) = 41  → Moderate band, middle of the range
   The model is not stuck in one band — it spans the full scale.

2. JUDGE IDENTITY MATTERS
   Presets 3 vs 4: same case type (juvenile), same district, different
   judge position and court seat → Moderate (41) vs High (57).
   The model distinguishes judges by their historical case durations.

3. COURT BACKLOG DRIVES VERY HIGH SCORES
   Preset 10 / 19 (r.c.a., Amalner, backlog 11,000) = 87 Very High
   Preset 12  (declarative suits, Nandurbar, backlog 10,200) = 100
   court_prior_active_backlog and court_prior_delay_rate are the
   throughput features that differentiate High from Very High.

4. CASE TYPE DOMINATES THE SHAP DRIVER LIST
   case_type_str is the top SHAP driver in every preset, with
   contributions ranging from −5.81 to −6.19. The model has learned
   strong case-type-specific risk patterns that are consistent across
   districts and judges.

5. THROUGHPUT IS SECONDARY, NOT DECISIVE, FOR MOST CASE TYPES
   Preset 11 (money suit, low backlog 1,100) = 81 Very High
   Preset 15 (probate, lowest backlog 800) = 81 Very High
   Even at the lowest-throughput courts, civil case types still land in
   the Very High band. The model's baseline for civil suits is high
   regardless of court throughput — a useful honest note for stakeholders.

6. REPRODUCIBILITY CONFIRMED
   Presets 10 and 19 are identical inputs and produce identical outputs
   (0.877, Very High). JDIS predictions are deterministic — the same
   case record always yields the same risk score.

7. GENDER IS NOT A SYSTEMATIC RISK FACTOR
   Presets 13, 14, 16, 18 have female party or advocate features.
   Gender features appear in the top-4 SHAP drivers only occasionally
   and with small magnitudes (−0.54 to +0.49). They are not driving
   the band assignment — the model's risk predictions are not
   systematically influenced by the gender of parties or advocates.

8. OOD PLATEAU AVOIDED
   All 20 presets use in-distribution OHE values, so each produces a
   differentiated, meaningful score rather than the flat ~0 score that
   out-of-distribution inputs would produce. This is a direct contrast
   to the 3 original frontend presets that all returned 0 because they
   used OOD values.
